\section{Related Work}
\subsection{ACT-R and Production Systems}

Traditional cognitive architectures such as ACT-R model reasoning as a sequence of production cycles in which working memory is matched against condition elements and a single production is selected for execution. This design has been highly successful for explaining efficient symbolic reasoning and bounded rationality in human cognition. However, the Match and Select stages are typically treated as implicit and highly optimized operations, so the actual dynamics of conflict resolution and retrieval are often left as implementation-level details rather than as explicit components of the cognitive model.

This makes the reasoning process appear largely serial, discrete, and linear. In contrast, Micro-ACT-R treats selection as an explicit search process over a structured topological potential landscape. Rather than assuming that production matching is an opaque black box, we model candidate states as spatially organized memory elements whose relative energy or attraction determines retrieval and adaptation.

\subsection{Structured Knowledge and Category-Theoretic Models}

A second line of work emphasizes structured representations of knowledge, including semantic graphs, category-theoretic accounts of cognition, and compositional memory architectures. These approaches highlight the importance of relations, transformations, and algebraic consistency in representing conceptual structure. They provide a strong foundation for modeling symbolic organization and relational reasoning, yet they often focus on the static arrangement of concepts rather than on the dynamics of explicit conflict resolution among competing states.

Micro-ACT-R is aligned with this structural perspective in that knowledge is organized through relations and categories. However, our framework extends this view by coupling representational structure with a dynamical potential field. In this sense, memory is not merely a symbolic graph; it is a topological substrate over which reasoning trajectories emerge, compete, and are corrected.

\subsection{Potential Fields, Graph Dynamics, and Quantum-Inspired Architectures}

Recent work in neural-symbolic systems, energy-based models, and graph-based reasoning has explored the use of potential functions, attractor dynamics, and relational structure to model cognition and optimization. These approaches suggest that cognitive processes may be interpreted as trajectories through a state space shaped by local energies and distributed constraints. Similarly, quantum-inspired models have introduced phase-based or probabilistic mechanisms for representing superposition, interference, and non-classical correlation.

Nevertheless, many of these frameworks either remain primarily optimization-oriented, or adopt quantum concepts as analogies rather than as structured cognitive mechanisms. Micro-ACT-R differs from these approaches by integrating three explicit components: a structured topological memory space, a potential-based dynamics for candidate selection, and a phase-coupled mechanism for coordination across distributed memory states. This combination aims to capture both the structural organization of knowledge and the non-linear character of reasoning under uncertainty.
